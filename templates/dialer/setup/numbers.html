How many numbers you need, and why we never release one you have called from

Local presence

People answer numbers that look like they are from nearby. If your leads are in the 423 area code, call them from a 423 number. The lift is real and it is large — often the difference between a one in ten answer rate and a one in five.

If you call several areas, hold one number per area and let the system pick the matching one. A number costs $1.15 a month, so four areas is $4.60 a month.

How many you need

Plan on one number for every 100 to 150 outbound calls a day. Carriers watch call volume per number. Push 400 dials a day through a single number and it gets labelled inside a week, no matter how polite your reps are.

Rep pool and AI pool

A number handed to ElevenLabs for the AI has its call delivery owned by ElevenLabs. It cannot also serve the rep dialer at the same time. That is why the pool is a choice, not a toggle.

Why releasing is nearly always a mistake

Once a number has made an outbound call, people will call it back — sometimes weeks later, often the ones most likely to buy. If you release it, Twilio gives it to another customer, and that callback reaches a stranger. The number is gone for good; there is no way to buy it back.

You are saving $1.15 a month. Keep the number. If you want to stop using it, let it rest instead: the system can park a number so it stops dialing out but still receives calls.

If a number starts showing as “Spam Likely”

  1. Stop dialing from it. More calls make the label stick harder.
  2. Finish the business verification on step 3 if you have not.
  3. Register the number with Voice Integrity, which is free once you are verified. It takes 24 to 48 hours.
  4. Buy a replacement number in the meantime and keep your daily volume per number down.
