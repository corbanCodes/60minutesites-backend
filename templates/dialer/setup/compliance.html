The four rules in plain English

1. Recording

Most states let you record a call if one person on it knows. Eleven states require everyone to know. The announcement at the start covers you in all of them, costs you three seconds, and almost nobody hangs up over it. If you are going to record at all, announce it.

2. Disclosure

If a machine is doing the talking, several states say the person has to be told in the first seconds of the call. The sentence previewed above does that. It also makes the call go better: people who find out halfway through get angry, and people told up front mostly carry on.

Read this twice: disclosure and consent are different things. Telling someone they are speaking with AI satisfies the disclosure laws. It does not give you permission to have called them. Permission has to exist before you dial.

3. Consent, and the cell phone line

Calling a cell phone with an automated or AI voice needs written permission from that person, beforehand. There is no version of this where you call cold cells with an AI and are fine. The penalty is counted per call, and the people who sue over it do it professionally.

So the system checks what kind of line every number is before an AI dials it, and refuses the cells unless there is a consent record on file. You can switch that check off; doing so is logged against your name with the words you typed to justify it.

A human rep calling a cell phone is a different matter and is generally fine, subject to hours and do-not-call lists.

4. Hours and lists

Eight in the morning to nine at night, in the time zone of the person you are calling. Several states are tighter and some forbid Sundays. Keep your own do-not-call list, and honour it the first time someone asks. The system adds anyone who asks to be left alone automatically, as soon as the transcript is read.

What this page does not do

It is not legal advice and it does not make you compliant. It is a set of defaults, cautiously chosen, and a log of what you changed. Before you point an AI at a cold list, pay a lawyer for an hour. One hour costs less than one complaint.
