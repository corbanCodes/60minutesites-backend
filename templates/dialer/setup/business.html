What you will see on Twilio, screen by screen

Twilio calls this a Customer Profile, not “business verification”, which is why the link above lands somewhere that looks unrelated. These are the choices that are hard to undo. Everything else on the form is just typing.

  1. Customer Profiles page → “Create Primary Customer Profile”. Leave Secondary Profiles alone. Those describe other people's businesses that you make calls for. You are calling for yourself.
  2. Profile type → Business. Twilio's own wording offers Individual “for sole proprietors”, and that is a trap. An Individual profile gets you unbranded calls only: no attestation A, no Voice Integrity, and a concurrency cap you cannot lift. Your sole proprietorship is a business. Pick Business.
  3. Business identity → Direct Customer. The option reading “My business uses Twilio to communicate internally or with our customers”. ISV Reseller or Partner is for selling Twilio inside a product your own customers use. Picking it changes the vetting path and buys you nothing here.
  4. Legal business name and address. Character for character as they appear on your registration, not your trading name and not a mailbox. This is the single most common reason a profile comes back.
  5. Company type. Pick the one that matches your registration. If the list offers only Corporation, LLC, Partnership, Co-operative and Non-profit with no sole-proprietor option, stop and ask Twilio support which to use rather than guessing, because you cannot change it after you submit. Whatever you pick here, the profile itself stays a Business profile.
  6. Business registration ID → your EIN. If you have never hired anyone you may not have one. An EIN is free from the IRS, the application takes about ten minutes and issues the number during the session. Get it before you start this form rather than halfway through. Nothing else will do: Voice Integrity and CNAM both reject any registration authority that is not an EIN or a DUNS number.
  7. Industry, website and regions of operation. A one-page site is fine.
  8. Authorized representative → you. Use the name exactly as it reads on your government ID, and a plain job title such as Owner. A playful title invites a manual review.
  9. Submit. The status turns to In Review and Twilio emails you.

Start this now, not later. Twilio quotes up to 48 hours, and a rejected submission restarts the clock. Everything else in this setup can carry on while you wait — you can connect numbers, write your script and make test calls with the profile still pending.

Proofread before you submit. You cannot edit it afterwards.

A profile in review is locked, and an approved one is locked too. The only version Twilio lets you change is a rejected one. Check the legal name, the registration number and the address against the paperwork before you press the button, because the alternative is waiting for a rejection in order to earn the right to fix a typo.

What actually gets profiles rejected

Twilio publishes its rejection codes. Almost all of them are the same mistake wearing different hats: something you typed does not match a record someone else holds.

  1. Your email is at a different domain than your website. This is the one that catches people, and a Gmail address next to a business website is exactly the pattern it looks for. Use an address at the same domain as the site you list.
  2. Your business name does not match your website. Twilio compares the legal name against the site and its SSL certificate. If you are registered under your own name but trade under a brand, those will not match on their own. Registering the trading name with your state so both read the same is the clean fix.
  3. Your legal name does not match your tax records. It is compared against the letter the IRS sent when it issued your EIN, character for character. A W-9 is not a reliable copy of that name.
  4. The registration number is formatted. Nine digits, nothing else. No dash, no spaces.
  5. The address is not one a database recognises. A real US street address with the country set to US. Missing fields fail the same way a wrong address does.

Rejections tend to arrive one problem at a time rather than as a list, and each round restarts the review clock. Getting it right once is worth the extra ten minutes.

After Twilio approves you

Approval on its own does not sign your calls. The profile proves who you are; two more free products apply that proof to specific numbers. The order is strict, and a number you skip will simply not appear in the next picker.

  1. Assign your outbound numbers to the approved business profile.
  2. Create the SHAKEN/STIR trust product, link it to that profile, assign the same numbers, submit. This is what actually produces attestation A.
  3. Create the Voice Integrity trust product the same way. This is the one that gets a “Spam Likely” label removed later. Allow another 24 to 48 hours.
  4. Optionally add CNAM so your name shows under your number on phones that display it.

Only relevant if you later split calling across Twilio subaccounts: each subaccount needs its own secondary profile carrying the same details, because a trust product has to live in the same account as the numbers it covers.

Why phone companies ask for this at all

Every US carrier now scores incoming calls and decides whether to label them. The score is built from who the carrier believes placed the call. If nobody has proved who you are, your calls are unsigned, and unsigned calls get labelled.

A business profile is how Twilio proves it to the carriers on your behalf. Once it is approved and you have attached your numbers to a SHAKEN/STIR trust product, Twilio signs those calls at the highest level, which is what the carriers call attestation A. Approval alone does not sign anything, which catches a lot of people out.

Timeline

  • Submitting: 10 to 15 minutes if you have the ID and the EIN ready.
  • Review: Twilio says up to 48 hours. In practice most clear the same business day.
  • If it is rejected: Twilio emails the reason. The usual cause is a business name that does not match the registration character for character, or an address that is a mailbox rather than a street address.

After approval

Two more things become available, both free, both worth doing on the numbers step:

  • Voice Integrity — registers your numbers with the analytics companies the carriers use. This is what you use to get a “Spam Likely” label removed. It takes another 24 to 48 hours.
  • CNAM — the caller name, up to 15 characters, that shows under your number on phones that display it.

If Twilio support asks what you are using the numbers for, answer plainly: outbound sales calls to business contacts, from a script, during business hours, with a do-not-call list. Vague answers are what get applications held.
